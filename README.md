## Overview

The Xtabay (also X'tabay or Xtabai) is a Yucatec Maya female spirit native to the folklore of the Yucatán Peninsula and adjacent areas of Mexico, Belize, and Guatemala. She is described as a beautiful woman dressed in white, with very long black hair reaching to her ankles and large dark eyes, who hides behind ceiba trees (Ceiba pentandra, sacred in Maya cosmology) on lonely forest paths at night. When a man — typically depicted as drunk, unfaithful, or generally dissolute — approaches, she beckons him into the forest; in different versions she causes disorientation and seduction, drives him mad, transforms into a serpent and devours him, throws him from a cliff, or removes his heart. The most widely circulated narrative origin, set down in Jesús Azcorra Alejos's Diez Leyendas Mayas (1998), pairs Xtabay with a counterpart figure: Xkeban, a woman judged immoral by society but compassionate toward the poor, becomes after death the fragrant xtabentún flower (Turbina corymbosa), while the outwardly virtuous but cruel Utz-Colel is reborn as the demonic Xtabay. The figure is etymologically linked to Ixtab, a pre-Columbian Maya goddess associated with hanging and the suicide-dead. In some regional versions — notably in Quintana Roo — Xtabay is reframed as a moral enforcer who punishes drunkards, adulterers, and violent men. The legend remains a living cautionary tradition across the Yucatán, used by parents and grandparents to warn against drinking, infidelity, and night-time wandering.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Yucatec Maya. The first element 'Ix' is a feminine prefix; the root 'tab' is glossed as 'rope' or 'cord (of specific use)', linking the figure to the Maya goddess Ixtab, deity of those who died by hanging.
- **Meaning**: Approximately 'the female one of the rope'; via the link to Ixtab, the figure is doubly tied to themes of suicide, hanging, and the noose-rope motif present in Maya cosmology.
- **First use**: Yucatec Maya oral tradition predating contact; written versions in Spanish and English appear in 19th- and 20th-century Mexican folklore collections, with Azcorra Alejos's Diez Leyendas Mayas (1998) being a widely cited modern reference.

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Forest spirit / restless dead** _(Yucatec Maya oral tradition)_

In Yucatec Maya tradition Xtabay is a real spirit being — a restless dead woman or supernatural entity bound to the ceiba tree — who acts on those who break moral and communal norms.

The Xtabay functions as a cautionary tale used to discourage drunkenness, infidelity, and night-wandering, mapped onto the broader Latin American family of female-revenant warning figures.
